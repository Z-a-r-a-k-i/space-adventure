## Purpose

This pack explores two separate alien-species questions:

- three visibly sapient peoples who could support conversation and social
  interaction in jungle, desert, and city settings; and
- three visibly non-sapient fauna directions with readable body-based threat
  surfaces in the same environments.

This separation supports the product rule that an eligible sapient character
should expose conversation, while a non-sapient creature does not require a
dialogue path. The sheets do not decide that any depicted species is friendly,
hostile, recruitable, playable, common, rare, or present in the current POC.

The current station POC remains bounded to its approved humans and security
machines. These alien directions do not add content to that roster or authorize
custom rigs, encounters, attacks, dialogue, factions, or production work.

## Review boundary

Review the sapient sheet for:

- three clearly different anatomies rather than three human costume variants;
- socially readable faces, manipulators, posture, clothing, and practical
  equipment;
- biome adaptation without assigning a species to one morality or faction;
- consistency with the project's stylized low-poly and retro-industrial
  material language; and
- whether each could support clear conversation staging and portrait framing.

Review the fauna sheet for:

- three clearly non-sapient and biologically coherent silhouettes;
- a reinforced, readable body-based attack surface without defining an attack;
- sufficient articulation for a possible wind-up, contact, rebound, and
  recovery;
- strong separation from robots, humanoids, and recognizable Earth animals;
  and
- tactical-camera readability without relying only on glowing eyes or color.

Approval of a sheet would authorize only species-level visual direction. It
would not define names, culture, biology, language, faction, equipment,
dialogue, aggression, combat rules, party eligibility, rig topology, or
implementation order. Any production species requires a later roster decision,
approved reference, accepted production-ready brief, assigned ownership, and
the normal licensing, Blender, Godot, and human-review gates.
