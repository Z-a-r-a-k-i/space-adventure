## Purpose

This pack explores, in product-value order, how the existing visual language
could communicate companion recruitment, dialogue consequences, two-person
exploration, sapient parley, station completion, life aboard the escape cutter,
and a bounded ship-repair crisis. It then compares the shared world language
and proposes peaceful arrival hubs for the jungle, desert, and city planets.

The sequence deliberately starts with the active party-and-conversation
identity rather than expanding the current gameplay roster. The station POC
remains authoritative. Planetary hubs, alien encounters, and ship combat remain
exploratory or roadmap-gated unless their owning design records say otherwise.

## Review boundary

Review the sequence for:

- whether recruitment reads as voluntary trust rather than a quest reward or
  forced assignment;
- whether a dialogue outcome is visible in the world without depending on UI;
- whether exactly two active party members remain readable at tactical camera
  distance;
- whether parley and machine-threat posture are immediately distinct;
- whether station escape reads as one authored transition rather than a
  generalized boarding system;
- whether the cutter supports two-person life and only the bounded
  weapons-engines-shields combat proof of concept;
- whether the two ships remain visibly separated, bows-up, and free of movement
  or trajectory lines;
- whether the station, planets, and cutter share a coherent construction and
  color language without becoming visually interchangeable; and
- whether each arrival hub promises conversation, repair, shelter, and
  exploration before combat.

Approval of an image would authorize only that visual direction. It would not
activate planet production, alien or companion rosters, abilities, dialogue
content, encounter logic, boarding, travel, generalized ship systems, asset
production, or gameplay implementation. Any production asset still requires
its normal roster decision, approved reference, accepted production-ready
brief, assigned ownership, licensing and privacy prerequisites, and direct
Blender and Godot review.
