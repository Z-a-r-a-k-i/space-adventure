# Prototype audit — 2026-09-05

The immediate priority is to make the existing solo fight controllable,
visually credible, and reliably inspectable before extending Phase 4 to the
Protector and sentry encounter. The core/presentation separation is useful and
should remain. Several concrete defects explain the current experience without
requiring an engine, architecture, or character-generation replacement.

Remote `main` was fetched. `HEAD` and `origin/main` both resolved to
`3ef703e5c13417fb84ab6a87af10596a923b7e65`, with zero commits ahead or behind.
The six pre-existing modified GLB import files were preserved. This audit changes
no gameplay code, scenes, content, or published assets. The temporary addon
settings and runtime weapon-scale experiment were restored, and the audit's
game/editor processes were closed. Nothing was committed or pushed.

**1. High priority: the carbine becomes approximately 52 times too small.**

In the running game, the weapon mesh has a local length of approximately 0.82 m,
but its world transform has a uniform scale of approximately 0.019069. Its
effective length is therefore 0.01564 m: about 1.6 cm. The weapon is present in
the scene tree, yet Vanguard looks almost empty-handed.

`VanguardPresentation.AttachWeapon` reparents the independently authored weapon
beneath a scaled imported rig socket and assigns `Transform3D.Identity`
([source](../game/scripts/VanguardPresentation.cs)). Socket existence alone
does not establish correct world size, orientation, or grip fit. An isolated
runtime scale compensation made the weapon visible; it was a diagnostic,
not a completed attachment repair.

First repair the attachment boundary so it preserves the weapon's metric size,
then fit the primary grip, support hand, shoulder, and barrel across armed idle,
walking, draw, fire, and holster. Validate world dimensions in the exact live
assembly, including after retry and attachment transfers. Correcting this is
necessary before judging whether the underlying rifle animations need replacing.

Evidence: ignored `artifacts/audit-20260905/weapon-transform.json`,
`weapon-scale-defect.png`, and `temporary-scale-probe.png`.

**2. High priority: repeated target orders can prevent firing or bypass recovery.**

These experiments used the running Godot scene, the ordinary JSON-to-typed
command adapter, and exact paused gameplay ticks. Each comparison began from a
reset solo encounter after its 42-tick ready phase.

| Input pattern | Advancement | Result |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| One basic attack order | 240 ticks / 8 s | 80 damage; enemy at 20 HP |
| Reissue the same target every 5 ticks | 240 ticks / 8 s | Zero damage; enemy at 100 HP |
| Reissue immediately after each release, every 10 ticks | 100 ticks / 3.33 s | 100 damage; encounter securing |

Every accepted assignment constructs a new attack action. Assignment replaces
the current action, resetting its phase. Consequently, rapid clicking repeatedly
restarts wind-up; well-timed clicking discards recovery. This is particularly
damaging to a game intended to reward tactical orders rather than click cadence.

Make repeat orders to the current target idempotent. Keep attack recovery debt
independent of action replacement, while explicitly deciding which movement,
retargeting, and ability cancellations are allowed. Add focused regressions for
both failure modes. The ordinary repeating cycle also includes a separate
one-tick phase transition beyond the authored wind-up and recovery; formalize
the intended cadence when making this repair.

Sources: [attack construction and progression](../src/SpaceAdventure.Core/GameSession.Combat.cs)
and [assignment/promotion](../src/SpaceAdventure.Core/GameSession.cs).
Reproduction: ignored `artifacts/audit-20260905/combat-probes.ps1` and its JSON.

**3. High priority: deterministic stepping does not reconstruct animation time.**

At the paused draw checkpoint, advancing 21 gameplay ticks reduced the ready
phase from 42 to 21 ticks remaining, while `AnimationPlayer` remained at time
0.0 before and after. The attachment transfer follows phase progress, but the
skeletal pose does not. This can move a weapon to the hand while the frozen
draw animation has not reached that pose.

`AdvanceExactTicks` and `AdvanceUntilEventJson` step the core. Presentation
selects clips and sets speed to zero while paused, without seeking to observed
phase progress. A resulting screenshot can therefore show the correct gameplay
state with an incorrect pose. Effects likewise use a separate real-time lifetime;
batch stepping can accumulate effects while paused instead of showing their
proper age at the inspected tick.

Add a small deterministic combat-review path: known scenario checkpoints,
action-instance identity, explicit phase/clip time, release/contact landmarks,
and effects aged against the same presentation time. Provide full-speed playback
of that sequence and a bounded recording for judging motion. Keep exact stepping
for rule diagnosis, but do not treat its current screenshots as animation proof.

Sources: [automation stepping](../game/scripts/AutomationBridge.cs),
[Vanguard synchronization](../game/scripts/VanguardPresentation.cs),
[humanoid synchronization](../game/scripts/HumanoidPresentation.cs), and
[effect processing](../game/scripts/GameHost.cs).

**4. High priority: combat feedback and interrupt timing need redesign.**

The tracer/muzzle flash starts at actor position plus a fixed 1.25 m vertical
offset. It never reads the actual published muzzle socket. The support-grip
socket is also unused by the runtime character assembly.

Measured published clip lengths refine the timing diagnosis: the rifle-fire
clip is already 0.3 s, so normal firing is approximately 1x, not a long clip
being sped up. However, the entire firing clip runs during the wind-up and is
replaced by armed idle at authoritative release. Suppressive Fire stretches
the same clip to 0.6 s. Draw compresses 2.93 s to 1.4 s, and holster compresses
3.3 s to 1.4 s. The Enforcer plays its strike only during the final nine ticks
of wind-up, then switches to idle at contact. These mappings provide little
opportunity for convincing follow-through and recovery.

Suppressive Fire requires 18 ticks against a 24-tick hostile wind-up. In the
live test, issuing it at wind-up start interrupted the enemy and prevented
damage. Waiting seven ticks before issuing it still dealt 30 damage but failed
to interrupt; Vanguard took 15 damage. The response budget is roughly 0.2 s,
and by the time the visible punch starts it is already too late. The current
red ring has no countdown or progress cue.

Use the real muzzle and coherent aim direction, stage recoil at release, retain
follow-through after contact, and give the interrupt a readable response window.
Add a progress telegraph and display cooldowns in seconds. A small authored
recoil/aim layer and a support-hand constraint are worthwhile experiments with
the current assets. Full-body donor clips need not dictate every part of the
weapon motion. Retiming authoritative values should be evaluated alongside the
visible motion rather than preserving the current numbers automatically.

Evidence: ignored `artifacts/audit-20260905/interrupt-probe.json`.
Sources: [combat presentation](../game/scripts/GameHost.cs),
[timing content](../game/content/station-route.json), and
[attack contract](ATTACK-PRESENTATION.md).

**5. Medium priority: ability and healing orders leave Vanguard idle afterward.**

Live tests confirmed that Field Aid and Suppressive Fire replace the repeating
attack, then clear the action after recovery. Vanguard needs another target
order to resume shooting. This follows the current single-primary-action design,
so it is a control-design finding rather than an undocumented rule failure.
The feedback says the ability/item is queued without explaining the lost attack
intent.

For this prototype, retain an explicit attack target across temporary abilities
and healing, with movement/cancel deliberately clearing it. Alternatively, keep
replacement semantics but make the interruption and idle outcome unmistakable.
Test that choice with the repaired same-target/recovery behavior before adding
the second controllable combatant.

**6. Medium priority: presentation obscures the prototype's useful information.**

Direct 1920×1080 Godot captures show the combat status text overflowing its
fixed panel, controls positioned around the middle of the screen, and a service
door lintel occluding Vanguard at the encounter entrance. The camera remains
where the player left it; the encounter does not frame the confrontation.
After recruitment the objective directs the player toward a main encounter
that is intentionally unimplemented, with the final airlock still unavailable.

Use a compact anchored HUD, health bars and action progress near the relevant
combatants, visible ability controls, and a deliberate combat-entry composition.
Extend cutaway handling to the obstructing doorway structure. Add an explicit
end-of-current-slice message after recruitment until the next encounter exists.
Simple shot/contact/interrupt/confirmation sounds would add useful feedback;
the current game-owned scripts/scenes contain no audio playback implementation.

Actor positions are assigned directly from 30 Hz gameplay observations while
clips run per rendered frame. Turning uses a fixed per-frame blend factor and
movement facing aims at the final destination rather than the current path
segment. Interpolated presentation positions, time-based turning, and facing
actual travel direction are targeted locomotion improvements worth testing.
These are code findings; this audit did not establish their perceived severity
through a continuous motion recording.

**Verification and practical limits.**

The canonical doctor, complete build, 23 core tests, CLI `station-route`, Godot
headless victory, and Godot headless defeat/retry checks ran successfully.
The live route reached recruitment, added Protector, and confirmed the final
airlock remained locked. Input injection produced an `input.attack.1` pending
order through the actual contextual click handler. Combat probes then used typed
orders and bounded stepping. None of this is an owner-operated physical playtest.

The development bridge reached the paused combat checkpoint in approximately
0.7–1.2 s, excluding engine startup. A five-second real-time combat sample
advanced 150 ticks and returned 30 process-delta samples near 16.7 ms. This did
not reproduce persistent game slowdown. These are short, smoothed engine-delta
samples, not a GPU profile, frame-time percentile benchmark, or proof of smoothness.

There are separate workstation/tool issues. The default PATH selects the system
.NET installation with SDK 8.0.421, while this project pins 8.0.319. The required
SDK already exists under `C:/Users/zaraki/.dotnet`; a process-local PATH and
DOTNET_ROOT selection resolved the check. NuGet vulnerability-feed warnings
persisted on an outside-sandbox rerun, although build/tests passed. Godot's
sandbox certificate-store errors disappeared outside the sandbox. The Windows
computer-use capture reported an app-approval timeout; direct Godot viewport
capture and inspection worked. Tool waiting should be measured separately from
game simulation and rendering.

The existing combat smoke's presentation assertions inspect visibility, death
action selection, pause state, and door state. They do not verify weapon world
size, grip contact, muzzle alignment, firing pose, or animation/event timing.
Passing those smokes therefore does not contradict the defects above.

**Recommended next slice.**

Repair weapon attachment and repeated-order semantics first, alongside a small
reproducible combat-review harness. Then evaluate one complete Vanguard/Enforcer
exchange with improved aim, release/contact, recovery, telegraph, and HUD feedback.
Use the existing assets for this first pass; replace particular donor clips or
repair weights only where the corrected assembly reveals a named defect. The
owner's permission to experiment supports alternatives to the present animation
recipe; preserve the pure core, typed commands, fixed ticks, and bounded roadmap.

Exit this repair slice when repeated clicks cannot change attack cadence,
weapon/grips remain credible through transitions and retry, paused review agrees
with real-time playback, the interrupt is understandable, and a fresh graphical
solo fight succeeds and fails predictably. Then implement Protector's bounded
kit and the sentry/main encounter to complete Phase 4. Defer new locations,
broader asset generation, inventory, procedural systems, ship combat, and broad
performance infrastructure until their roadmap gates are reached.
